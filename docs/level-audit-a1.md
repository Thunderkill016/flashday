# A1 course level audit — issue #8

Audited `src/content/a1/` on 2026-09-27: all 30 lessons, including every pattern example, chunk target and example, drill prompt and option, dialogue line, and listening text. English task models were also spot checked. Vietnamese instructions, distractors deliberately showing _wrong grammar_, proper names, place names, brands, spelled letters, and numerals are outside the vocabulary counts. Locations below use `P` (pattern), `T` (chunk target), `E` (chunk example), `D` (drill), `R` (reading dialogue), and `L` (listening). A location means at least one occurrence, not an occurrence count.

## Reference and limits

- Vocabulary comparator: [Oxford 3000 by CEFR level, Oxford University Press](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/external/pdf/wordlists/oxford-3000-5000/The_Oxford_3000_by_CEFR_level.pdf), downloaded 2026-09-27; PDF SHA-256 `b81e3f65c976fc93eb342ba34c40f9c648e461cd3`. A1/A2/B1/B2 below mean the _headword_ in that PDF. `U` means **unlisted**, not “above B2.” Inflections are checked against their lemma. This is a screening proxy, not an EVP sense-level lookup; in particular `take`, `get`, `feel`, and `make` can form harder phrases despite A1 headwords. The [Oxford wordlist explanation](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/about/wordlists/oxford3000-5000) describes its CEFR tagging.
- Grammar comparator: [English Grammar Profile](https://englishprofile.org/?menu=english-grammar-profile) and [English Profile reference-level booklet](https://www.englishprofile.org/images/pdf/theenglishprofilebooklet.pdf). EGP distinguishes _form and meaning_, so this report does not assign a level to a form merely because its words are A1. The booklet includes future `be going to` and past-event clauses in A2 examples. Entries below marked `A2 boundary` are a curriculum judgment, not a claimed exact EGP entry. [EVP](https://englishprofile.org/?menu=english-vocabulary-profile) is sense and phrase based, but no bulk EVP export was used; these are **not** EVP-certified levels.
- `U` includes local realia (pho, motorbike), proper contexts, and potentially useful transparent compounds. An isolated `U` in a translated context is weaker evidence than a new, opaque _chunk target_ or several unknown words in one listening sentence. Medical passages here are a language-level concern; this audit makes no clinical-safety judgment.

The six checkpoint rows were compared with the **four earlier lessons of the same stage**, not merely with the course as a whole. New words in a realistic scene may be harmless; new _assessed chunk targets_ conflict with `REBUILD_A1.md` §3 and `schema.js`'s checkpoint comment. The strongest material problems are s4-l5's B1/B2 target cluster, s5-l1's dense unintroduced medical language, and the checkpoint reuse contract across stages. `be going to` is the planned s6-l1 A2-boundary lesson and reappears in the s6-l5 drill; other A2-boundary grammar occurs in s6-l3 (past simple), and `studied` appears earlier in s5-l2 listening. Therefore it is **not the only** above-A1 grammar pattern in the course.

No course content was changed in this audit. Oxford headword labels cannot establish the level of every _sense_, and the EGP public category page does not provide a bulk, machine-readable passage classifier. A follow-up content edit should verify each replacement against the EVP sense/phrase entry manually and preserve the local communicative can-do.
